# Differentiable Inverse Problems

## 1. Data Boundary

The inverse scripts use synthetic current targets generated by a
high-resolution NumPy ProductIntegral-DtN solver. The inverse model is the
lower-resolution differentiable PyTorch solver. The target and inverse grids
are deliberately different.

FDM is not imported, opened, or evaluated by either inverse script. FDM is
reserved for independent posterior forward validation.

## 2. Positive Bounded Parameterization

For a free positive parameter $p_j$ with numerical search bounds
$p_{j,\min}$ and $p_{j,\max}$, optimization uses an unconstrained variable
$z_j$:

$$
p_j(z_j)
=\exp\left[
\log p_{j,\min}
+(\log p_{j,\max}-\log p_{j,\min})
\frac{1}{1+e^{-z_j}}
\right].
$$

The current defaults are

$$
0.01<k_{\mathrm{cat}}<100,
\qquad
0.1<\gamma<100,
\qquad
0.005<\delta<0.2.
$$

These are inverse-search bounds, not a claim that all positive parameters are
equally identifiable.

## 3. Multi-Scan Objective

For scan $s$, let $\mathbf I_s(\mathbf p)$ be the predicted current samples and
$\mathbf I_s^{\mathrm{obs}}$ the observations. The clean current span is

$$
\Delta I_s
=\max_n I_{s,n}^{\mathrm{obs}}
-\min_n I_{s,n}^{\mathrm{obs}}.
$$

Each scan contributes equally through

$$
\mathcal L(\mathbf p)
=\frac{1}{N_s}
\sum_{s=1}^{N_s}
\frac{1}{N_t-1}
\sum_{n=1}^{N_t-1}
\left[
\frac{I_s(t_n;\mathbf p)-I_{s,n}^{\mathrm{obs}}}{\Delta I_s}
\right]^2.
$$

The separately imposed initial current is excluded. Noise is scaled by each
curve's clean span before being added.

## 4. Sensitivity and Scan Design

For scan $s$, the relative sensitivity matrix is

$$
S_s
=\frac{1}{\Delta I_s}
\left[
\frac{\partial\mathbf I_s}{\partial\log k_{\mathrm{cat}}}
\quad
\frac{\partial\mathbf I_s}{\partial\log\gamma}
\quad
\frac{\partial\mathbf I_s}{\partial\log\delta}
\right].
$$

The multi-scan matrix is the vertical stack of all $S_s$. The design script
uses exact PyTorch Jacobian-vector products, column-normalizes the matrix, and
ranks scan-rate sets by conditioning and sensitivity magnitude.

Wide scan-rate separation changes the relative electrode, film, reaction, and
external-memory time scales. Nearby scan rates mainly repeat the same
information.

## 5. Supported Inverse Modes

The multi-scan driver supports:

- `all`: infer $k_{\mathrm{cat}},\gamma,\delta$;
- `fix-gamma`: infer $k_{\mathrm{cat}},\delta$;
- `fix-k`: infer $\gamma,\delta$;
- `fix-delta`: infer $k_{\mathrm{cat}},\gamma$;
- `k-only`, `gamma-only`, and `delta-only` fallbacks.

Multi-start agreement is required for a credible joint result. A low objective
alone is not evidence of identifiability.

## 6. Structural Limits

### Weak reaction

When $C_{C,i}\approx\gamma$ and $\mathrm{Da}\ll1$,

$$
J\approx k_{\mathrm{cat}}C_{B,i}\gamma.
$$

The current primarily sees the product $k_{\mathrm{cat}}\gamma$, producing a
long inverse valley. Independent knowledge of $\gamma$ is the preferred first
fallback.

### Transport saturation

For large local $\mathrm{Da}_i$,

$$
\frac{\mathrm{Da}_i}{1+\mathrm{Da}_i}\longrightarrow1,
$$

and the local kinetic sensitivity tends to zero:

$$
\left.
\frac{\partial\log J}{\partial\log k_{\mathrm{cat}}}
\right|_{C_{B,s},C_{C,i},\delta}
=\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{Da}_i}
\longrightarrow0.
$$

In this regime, no optimizer or neural network can recover information absent
from the current. Additional observables, priors, or independently fixed
parameters are required.

## 7. Current Interpretation

The present evidence supports three-parameter inversion only in an interior,
sensitivity-qualified region. Every reported joint inverse should include:

- local singular values and parameter correlations;
- several widely separated starts;
- target/inverse resolution mismatch;
- noise and discretization audits;
- a regime-aware fallback when the Jacobian is nearly rank deficient.
