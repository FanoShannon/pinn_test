# Joint k/gamma/delta Forward and Delta-Inversion Study

## Scope and data separation

This study extends the zero-training coupled ProductIntegral-finite-slab-DtN-
TraceGreen operator to runtime thin-layer thickness:

```text
(k_cat, gamma, delta) -> concentrations, interface state, reaction flux, CV
```

No FDM value is used in an operator equation, loss, optimizer, checkpoint, or
inverse objective. FDM v4.2 remains an independent posterior reference and
error audit. Synthetic inversion targets are generated by a high-resolution
physical operator, not by FDM.

## Physics parameterization

The thin coordinate is fixed by

```text
s = x / delta,  0 <= s <= 1,
```

while the external coordinate is

```text
y = x - delta.
```

The finite-slab eigenvalues and the principal dimensionless groups are

```text
mu_m = (m + 1/2) pi / delta,
lambda_m = D_B mu_m^2,
Da = k_cat gamma delta / D_B,
Fo_T = D_B T_sim / delta^2.
```

At each time cell the coupled scalar closure remains

```text
J_n = k_cat C_B,int(J_n) C_C,int(J_n).
```

The runtime flux scale is

```text
J_ref = k_cat gamma / (1 + k_cat gamma delta / D_B).
```

No `dC/ddelta` term is added to the PDE. During inversion, automatic
differentiation is applied to the complete discrete solution map.

## Numerical changes

1. `delta` is passed explicitly through the finite-slab spectrum, field
   reconstruction, inventory current, external origin, FDM validation, and
   `J_ref`.
2. The TraceGreen transformed integral supports Gauss-Legendre quadrature.
   Sixty-four Gauss points reach approximately the accuracy of 512 uniform
   midpoint points.
3. FDM posterior diagnostics now distinguish the electrode-gradient current
   from the current reconstructed through the thin-layer mass balance:

   ```text
   J_e = -R - d/dt integral_0^delta C_B dx.
   ```

4. A float64 PyTorch implementation differentiates through the complete
   ProductIntegral-DtN recurrence and the unrolled nonlinear closure.
5. The triangular scan period is a runtime quantity, enabling multi-scan
   sensitivity studies.

## Forward convergence without FDM

The convergence audit used

```text
delta = 0.01, 0.0175, 0.035, 0.07, 0.14
k_cat = 1
gamma = 10
```

against a `4097 time x 256 mode` or finer operator reference.

- Concentration errors are primarily controlled by the temporal grid.
- Surface-current errors also depend on modal resolution because the boundary
  derivative weights mode `m` by `mu_m`.
- Reaction closure stayed below `6.2e-13`.
- A 100-case stress grid covering
  `Da = 1e-5 ... 1.4e3` produced no NaN, invalid concentration, or wrong
  nonlinear root. Newton required at most five iterations.

Core solve timings on the local CPU, excluding file I/O and posterior plots:

| Resolution | Median time |
|---|---:|
| NumPy `1025 x 128` | 0.072 s |
| NumPy `4096 x 256` | 0.508 s |
| PyTorch forward/backward `129 x 64` | 0.311 s |
| PyTorch forward/backward `257 x 96` | 0.676 s |

## Delta posterior

Independent FDM v4.2 cases use `k_cat=1`, `gamma=10`, 400 thin cells, 1000
external cells, and at least 8000 time points. The operator uses
`4096 time x 256 modes` and 64-point Gauss-Legendre TraceGreen quadrature.

| delta | Overall dimensionless RMSE | C_B RMSE | C_B interface RMSE | C_C interface/gamma RMSE | CV/J_ref RMSE |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0100 | 8.844e-6 | 1.936e-6 | 3.350e-6 | 1.343e-5 | 3.388e-5 |
| 0.0175 | 8.933e-6 | 2.997e-6 | 5.185e-6 | 1.395e-5 | 2.903e-5 |
| 0.0350 | 1.004e-5 | 1.815e-6 | 2.995e-6 | 2.031e-5 | 1.015e-5 |
| 0.0700 | 9.337e-6 | 7.646e-6 | 1.240e-5 | 1.379e-5 | 2.168e-5 |
| 0.1400 | 1.110e-5 | 1.289e-5 | 1.683e-5 | 1.228e-5 | 8.763e-5 |

The earlier 64-point midpoint TraceGreen overall errors were `1.033e-4` at
`delta=0.01` and `5.782e-5` at `delta=0.14`. Gauss-Legendre reduced them by
approximately 91% and 81%, respectively.

The quadrature choice is also supported without FDM: relative to an internal
512-point Gauss reference, 64-point Gauss field RMSE is `5.39e-7` at
`delta=0.01` and `2.21e-7` at `delta=0.14`.

## Joint posterior checks

Five independent three-parameter cases were added:

| k_cat | gamma | delta | Overall dimensionless RMSE | CV span NRMSE | CV/J_ref RMSE |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.1 | 0.1 | 0.01 | 1.255e-6 | 1.661e-4 | 3.329e-3 |
| 0.1 | 0.1 | 0.14 | 1.916e-5 | 1.997e-4 | 5.476e-2 |
| 10 | 0.1 | 0.14 | 3.394e-5 | 1.852e-4 | 6.072e-4 |
| 0.1 | 100 | 0.14 | 5.065e-6 | 9.087e-5 | 9.018e-5 |
| 10 | 10 | 0.14 | 6.474e-6 | 6.112e-5 | 5.955e-5 |

The large `CV/J_ref` number in the low-reaction thick case is a scaling
effect, not a large CV shape error. There `J_ref` is approximately `0.01`,
whereas the reversible thin-layer current spans approximately `2.74`.

### Low-flux current error decomposition

For `(k_cat, gamma, delta) = (0.1, 0.1, 0.14)`:

| Comparison | RMSE/J_ref |
|---|---:|
| FDM electrode gradient vs FDM thin balance | 5.547e-2 |
| Operator surface current vs FDM electrode gradient | 5.012e-2 |
| Operator surface current vs FDM thin balance | 1.196e-2 |

The operator-vs-balance error is only `4.36e-5` relative to the full CV span.
Thus most of the apparently large `J/J_ref` posterior error comes from the
FDM electrode-gradient discretization. Both current definitions remain
reported; neither is silently discarded.

## Differentiable delta inversion

The PyTorch recurrence matches the NumPy recurrence to approximately `1e-11`.
The automatic derivative of a CV objective with respect to `log(delta)`
matches centered finite differences within `2e-5` relative error.

Targets use a `4097 x 512` NumPy operator. Inversion uses only 257 current
samples and a `257 x 96` differentiable operator, starting from `delta=0.035`.

| True delta | Estimate, no noise | Relative error | Estimate, 0.1% noise | Relative error |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0175 | 0.01753295 | 0.188% | 0.01752657 | 0.152% |
| 0.0350 | 0.03505570 | 0.159% | 0.03507680 | 0.219% |
| 0.0700 | 0.07007069 | 0.101% | 0.07006476 | 0.093% |
| 0.1400 | 0.14011487 | 0.082% | 0.14012304 | 0.088% |

For `delta=0.07`, six initial values from `0.006` to `0.18` converged to
`0.0700706 ... 0.0700708`.

With only 65 CV samples:

| CV noise relative to span | Worst delta error |
|---:|---:|
| 0.5% | 1.01% |
| 1.0% | 1.42% |

## Joint inverse identifiability

A single CV is not sufficient for an unrestricted simultaneous
`(k_cat, gamma, delta)` inverse problem. At `delta=0.035`:

| Scan rates | Jacobian condition number | corr(log k, log gamma) | corr(log k, log delta) | corr(log gamma, log delta) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 | 69.6 | 0.9906 | -0.9924 | -0.9756 |
| 20, 40, 80 | 49.9 | 0.9850 | -0.9843 | -0.9543 |
| 5, 40, 320 | 14.8 | 0.9451 | -0.8838 | -0.7887 |

Wide scan-rate separation helps identify `delta`, but `k_cat` and `gamma`
remain strongly correlated. The supported conclusion is therefore:

- single-parameter `delta` inversion with fixed `k_cat` and `gamma` is viable;
- joint inversion needs additional concentration/interface observations,
  informative priors, or independently known `gamma`;
- a neural network cannot repair structural non-identifiability.

## Current limitations

- The Abel completed-history evaluation is still `O(N_t^2)`.
- The differentiable nonlinear solve uses a fixed unrolled Newton iteration.
- The verified forward thickness interval is `0.01 <= delta <= 0.14`; the API
  accepts any positive value, but accuracy outside this range is not claimed.
- The reduction currently requires `D_A=D_B` and `D_C=D_D`.
- No experimental inverse result has yet been included.
- FDM raw arrays are intentionally not tracked in Git.
